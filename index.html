            🌍 What is Climate Change?

            Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While some climate change occurs naturally, since the 1800s, human activities — especially burning fossil fuels — have been the main driver of climate change.

            The greenhouse effect is a natural process that keeps Earth warm. Greenhouse gases (GHGs) like carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O) trap heat in the atmosphere. Human activities have dramatically increased GHG concentrations, enhancing this effect and warming the planet.

            🔬 Key Greenhouse Gases

            • Carbon Dioxide (CO₂): Released by burning coal, oil, and gas. Stays in the atmosphere for hundreds of years.
            • Methane (CH₄): Produced by livestock, landfills, and natural gas leaks. More potent than CO₂ but shorter-lived.
            • Nitrous Oxide (N₂O): Emitted from agriculture, soils, and fertilizers.
            • Water Vapour: A natural amplifier — as Earth warms, more water evaporates, enhancing warming.

            🏭 Main Causes of Climate Change

            🔥 Burning Fossil Fuels

            Coal, oil, and natural gas release CO₂ and other GHGs when burned for electricity, heat, and transport. This is the largest source of global emissions.

            🌲 Deforestation

            Trees absorb CO₂. When forests are cleared for agriculture or urban development, stored carbon is released and the planet loses a crucial carbon sink.

            🐄 Agriculture & Food Systems

            Livestock (especially cattle) produce methane during digestion. Rice paddies, fertilizers, and food waste also contribute significantly.

            🏗️ Industry & Manufacturing

            Cement, steel, and chemical production release large amounts of CO₂ and other GHGs, often through chemical reactions that cannot easily be replaced.

            🗑️ Waste & Landfills

            Decomposing organic waste in landfills releases methane. Burning waste adds CO₂ and toxic pollutants to the air.

            🏙️ Urbanisation

            Cities replace natural vegetation with heat-absorbing surfaces (concrete, asphalt), creating "urban heat islands" and increasing local temperatures.

            🌪️ Effects of Climate Change

            🌡️ Extreme Heat

            More frequent and intense heatwaves affect health, agriculture, and outdoor workers.

            🌊 Flooding

            Heavier rainfall events and rising sea levels increase the frequency and severity of floods.

            🏜️ Drought

            Changing rainfall patterns cause prolonged droughts affecting water supplies and crop yields.

            🦋 Biodiversity Loss

            Shifting habitats and extreme events threaten plant and animal species worldwide.

            🌾 Agriculture

            Unpredictable seasons, heat stress, and water scarcity reduce crop yields and food security.

            🏥 Public Health

            Heat-related illness, spread of diseases, air pollution, and mental health impacts all increase.

            Climate change affects communities differently. Those with fewer resources — including many farming communities, coastal populations, and low-income households — often face the greatest challenges.

            ☁️ Weather vs. Climate

            People often confuse weather and climate. Understanding the difference is important:

            ☀️ Weather

            • Short-term atmospheric conditions
            • Hours to days timescale
            • Example: "It's raining today"
            • Changes frequently
            • Measured with thermometers, rain gauges, barometers

            🌍 Climate

            • Long-term average conditions
            • Decades to centuries timescale
            • Example: "This region gets monsoon rains every June"
            • Changes slowly over time
            • Studied through long-term data and climate models

            Simple way to remember: Climate is what you expect; weather is what you get.

            A single cold winter does not disprove climate change — scientists look at trends across decades to understand how the climate is changing.

            💪 What Can Individuals and Communities Do?

            While governments and industries must lead large-scale change, individuals and communities also play an important role.

            🚶

            Move Sustainably

            Walk, cycle, or use public transport when possible.

            💡

            Save Energy

            Switch off lights, use energy-efficient appliances, consider solar if accessible.

            🥗

            Eat Thoughtfully

            Reducing meat consumption and food waste can lower your carbon footprint.

            ♻️

            Reduce & Reuse

            Buy less, reuse what you have, separate recyclable waste where facilities exist.

            🌳

            Plant & Protect Trees

            Participate in local tree-planting drives and protect existing green spaces.

            📢

            Spread Awareness

            Talk to family and friends. Support local environmental initiatives and policies.

            Remember: not everyone has equal access to the same options. The goal is to do what you reasonably can within your circumstances, and advocate for systemic changes that make sustainable choices accessible to all.
